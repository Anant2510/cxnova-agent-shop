How to make a home insurance claim: 9 tips from our adjusters

By Maya Okafor, Senior Claims Adjuster. Published 12 September 2026, updated 2 October 2026. 7 minute read.

Most home claims go smoothly. The ones that stall usually stall for the same few reasons: damage that was not documented, a late report, or a repair that started before anyone could see the problem. These nine tips come from the adjusters who handle more than 40,000 home claims a year at Meridian Mutual.

What should I do in the first 24 hours?

1. Make the home safe. Turn off the water at the main valve if a pipe has burst, and switch off electricity to wet rooms if you can do it safely. Keep children and pets away from damaged areas.

2. Report the claim the same day. You do not need every detail to start. Members who report a claim within 24 hours are paid nine days sooner on average, because we can send help before the damage spreads.

3. Stop the damage getting worse. Cover a broken window, put a bucket under a leak and move belongings to a dry room. Reasonable emergency costs are covered, so keep the receipts.

What should I photograph?

Inside the home

4. Photograph every affected room before you clean up. Start with a wide shot from the doorway, then move in for close-ups of stains, water lines and damaged items. A two-minute video walk-through is just as good.

Outside the home

5. Record the outside too. Roof tiles, gutters, fences and the height of any standing water on the street help us confirm the cause quickly.

How do I report the claim?

6. Have four things ready: your policy number, when the damage happened, what you think caused it and a rough list of what was damaged. You can start a claim online or call our claims line on (800) 555-0199, which is open 24 hours a day.

How long will my claim take?

Most simple claims are settled within 10 days. According to the Home Claims Benchmark 2026, the industry median for a straightforward home claim is 17 days, and complex water and fire claims can take several months.

7. Answer requests quickly. The biggest single cause of delay is waiting for information, such as a repair estimate or a photo of a serial number. Reply to your adjuster within a day or two and your claim keeps moving.

What could slow my claim down?

8. Keep damaged items until we have seen them. If something must go for health reasons, photograph it first and keep a sample, such as a cut-out of carpet. For storm claims we check the date against the National Weather Data Service storm events database, so note when the damage happened.

Do not start permanent repairs before your adjuster agrees the scope. Emergency repairs to stop further damage are always fine.

Do I need a contractor’s estimate before I call?

9. Call us first. We can send an approved contractor, and work done by our network is guaranteed for five years. If you prefer your own contractor, the Consumer Insurance Council guide to documenting a claim explains what an estimate should include.

Quick answers

Will making a claim increase my premium?

A single weather claim rarely changes your price. Several claims in a short period can.

Can I choose my own contractor?

Yes. We will agree the cost with them before work starts.

What if I disagree with the settlement?

Ask your adjuster for a review. If you are still unhappy, our complaints team will look at it independently.

Is a burst pipe covered?

Yes, on every plan. Gradual leaks and wear and tear are not covered.
